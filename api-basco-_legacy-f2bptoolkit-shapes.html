basco._legacy.f2bptoolkit.shapes

Legacy compatibility reference. Prefer current BASCO workflows; copied implementation details are not new development targets.

Truth cubes from shape models: ellipsoid closed form, or a polyhedral mesh.

The estimator needs one thing from a shape model: the inertia integrals T^{lmn} = integral of x^l y^m z^n dm over the body, up to the active gravity order. For a uniform triaxial ellipsoid those are closed-form. For a mesh they are computed here, exactly, by summing signed tetrahedra.

Why this module exists rather than calling the C++ poly_inertia_met

The kernel already builds a mesh's moments, and for propagation that is the right routine to use. It is not usable as a TRUTH SOURCE for a covariance study, for two reasons:

  1. It takes the absolute value of the tetrahedron determinant (src/gubas_core.cpp). That is correct only when the mesh is star-shaped about the origin -- every facet's tetrahedron then has the same orientation. For a mesh that is not, the exterior slivers must CANCEL, and they only cancel if the sign is kept. The shipped Dimorphos mesh already violates star-shapedness in one facet of six thousand, so this is not hypothetical.
  2. It is reachable only through a full simulation config, which makes it awkward to test in isolation and impossible to use before a system is built.

The routine below keeps the sign, is exact for any closed mesh, and is checked against a body whose moments are known analytically.

Conventions, each of which is a choice and is therefore stated

MASS. The measured mass is held and the density follows from the mesh volume; the alternative -- hold the density, let the mass follow -- would silently change the total mass that every other quantity in the study depends on. The two differ by about ten per cent for Didymos, because the ellipsoid model is a bounding-box fit rather than a volume-equivalent one.

FRAME. Meshes are translated to their centre of mass and rotated to principal axes, because that is the frame the estimator and the coefficient converter both assume (and GUBAS carries only the diagonal of the inertia tensor, so an unaligned mesh is not representable in the rotational dynamics at all).

SIGN. Principal axes are determined only up to sign: there are four right-handed choices, and the ODD moments change sign between them. The convention is pinned deterministically -- see _pin_axis_signs. Without this the degree-3 truth values depend on the order the vertices happen to appear in the file.

_tet_monomial

_tet_monomial(a, b, c, p, q, r)

integral of x^p y^q z^r dV over the tetrahedron (0, a, b, c), SIGNED.

Substituting x = u a + v b + w c maps the tetrahedron onto the unit simplex with Jacobian det[a b c]; each coordinate power then expands multinomially, and the only integral needed is Dirichlet's

integral over the simplex of u^i v^j w^k = i! j! k! / (i+j+k+3)! .

The determinant is kept SIGNED. Summed over a closed surface the signed tetrahedra telescope to the enclosed volume regardless of whether the origin lies inside the body; with abs they do not.

_pin_axis_signs

_pin_axis_signs(vertices, faces)

Choose one of the four right-handed principal frames, deterministically.

An eigendecomposition fixes the principal AXES but not their directions. Flipping any two axes keeps the frame right-handed, so four frames are admissible, and the odd moments change sign between them: whether T^{300} comes out positive or negative would otherwise depend on the order the vertices appear in the file. Didymos is the worst case -- its two smaller principal moments differ by about one per cent, so even the AXES are numerically touchy.

The convention adopted: take the frame with T^{300} >= 0 and T^{030} >= 0. It is always reachable, because the three axis-pair flips generate all four sign patterns of (T^{300}, T^{030}).

truth_cube(system, order: int) -> Tuple[Dict[str, Dict[Slot, float]], dict]

Both bodies' cubes for a SystemConfig, honouring its shape selection.

Returns ({"A": cube, "B": cube}, provenance). The two bodies are independent: either, both or neither may be a mesh.
