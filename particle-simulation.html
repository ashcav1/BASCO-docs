How the particle simulation works

This page follows a particle from a row of a table to a line on a figure, and says which file does each step. Read it next to the code: every heading names the file to open.

The code is in src/basco/particles/. The shortest complete script is examples/particles/1_launch_from_surface.py.

What is computed

Two bodies (Didymos and Dimorphos) move under their mutual gravity and rotate. A particle is a point with no mass of its own. It feels the gravity of both bodies, and optionally the Sun; it does not move the bodies, and particles do not feel each other. The propagation of a particle ends when it reaches a body's surface. There is no bouncing.

The script, line by line

from basco import particles as bp

environment = bp.didymos_environment(state="AC2")
site = environment.site("Dimorphos", latitude_deg=0.0, longitude_deg=180.0)
initial_conditions = bp.read_initial_conditions("data/milani_bed1/dimorphos.csv")
trajectories = bp.propagate(environment, initial_conditions, site=site, duration_s=24 * 3600)
bp.plot_trajectories(trajectories, frame="Dimorphos", file="trajectories.png")
Line What you get File
didymos_environment The binary, its two shape models and the gravity model environment.py
environment.site A point on a body's surface with east, north and up axes sites.py
read_initial_conditions A table: one position and one velocity per particle initial_conditions.py
propagate The trajectory and the outcome of every particle trajectories.py
plot_trajectories A figure plots/figures.py

Underneath propagate:

What File
The propagator: integration steps and event detection propagator.py
The equations of motion dynamics.py
Solar gravity and radiation pressure (optional) solar.py
The small classes the propagator takes and returns state.py

Frames and units

Everything is in SI units (metres, seconds, kilograms), with one exception named below.

Frame Origin Axes
Inertial The binary's barycentre Fixed in space
Body-fixed, one per body The body's centre of mass The body's principal axes; they rotate with the body
Site A point on the surface x east, y north, z up (the surface normal averaged over a small patch)
Rotating The barycentre x from the primary to the secondary, z along the orbit's angular momentum

The exception is the binary's own state, which is stored the way GUBAS stores it: 30 numbers called the GUBAS state (src/basco/binary/state.py).

Entries Meaning Unit
0 to 2 Position of the secondary relative to the primary, in the primary's frame km
3 to 5 Velocity of the secondary relative to the primary, in the primary's frame km/s
6 to 8 Spin of the primary, in the primary's frame rad/s
9 to 11 Spin of the secondary, in the primary's frame rad/s
12 to 20 Rotation matrix from the primary's frame to the inertial frame, row by row none
21 to 29 Rotation matrix from the secondary's frame to the primary's frame, row by row none

body_poses in src/basco/binary/poses.py turns those 30 numbers into what the particle code uses: for each body its centre c [m], its velocity [m/s], its body-to-inertial rotation matrix C and its spin vector. The barycentre stays at the origin, so with r the relative position and m1, m2 the masses, the centres are -m2 / (m1 + m2) r and +m1 / (m1 + m2) r.

Step 1. The environment (environment.py)

didymos_environment(state="AC2") builds an Environment:

  • The masses and the initial state are those of the Hera paper (data/hera_paper), for one of its four rotation states AC0 to AC3 (src/basco/binary/didymos.py).
  • The shape models are data/shapes/didymos.obj and dimorphos.obj, moved so that the centre of mass is at the origin and the principal axes are x, y and z.
  • The inertia integrals of each body, which the binary's mutual gravity needs, are computed from the same shape models. The binary and the particles therefore see the same two objects.
  • gravity chooses the gravity of each body on a particle:
gravity= Model Valid
"polyhedral" (default) Constant-density polyhedron of the shape model (Werner and Scheeres) Everywhere outside the surface
"hou" The series used for the binary's mutual gravity Outside the body's Brillouin sphere only
"hou_no_brillouin_check" The same series, evaluated inside that sphere too Not guaranteed inside; for comparisons
"point" A point mass Everywhere

The Brillouin sphere of a body is the smallest sphere about its centre of mass that contains the whole body. A series in powers of 1/r converges only outside it, so a particle launched from the surface needs the polyhedral model.

Step 2. The site (sites.py)

environment.site("Dimorphos", latitude_deg, longitude_deg) finds where that direction from the body's centre meets the surface and builds the east-north-up frame there. "Up" is the surface normal averaged over a patch of 10 m radius, so that one tilted facet does not tilt the whole frame. Latitude is measured from the body's x-y plane toward +z, longitude from +x toward +y.

Step 3. The initial conditions (initial_conditions.py)

A table with one row per particle: position x_m, y_m, z_m and velocity vx_m_s, vy_m_s, vz_m_s. It comes from two arrays in your script or from a CSV file. The table does not know its frame. You state the frame when you propagate:

  • site=site: the site frame, with velocities relative to the ground;
  • body="Dimorphos": that body's fixed frame, with velocities relative to the rotating body;
  • neither: the inertial frame.

Environment.inertial_states converts the table to inertial states. For a point p moving at u in a body's frame,

r = c + C p
v = v_body + dC/dt p + C u

where dC/dt p is the velocity of the rotating ground under the point. A site adds one step before that: p = site point + E s, with s the position in the site frame and E the matrix whose columns are east, north and up.

Step 4. Batches (trajectories.py, propagate)

The particles are sorted by speed and cut into batches of batch_size (4 by default). One batch is one integration: one copy of the binary and the particles of that batch, all advanced with the same steps. Batches are independent, so with workers=8 eight of them run at the same time in separate processes. With checkpoint=folder, each finished batch is written to that folder and an interrupted run continues from there.

Because the particles of a batch share their step sizes, results change in the last digits when the batching changes. batch_size=1 removes that dependence and is slower.

Step 5. The equations of motion (dynamics.py)

The state vector of one batch is the GUBAS state of the binary followed by six numbers per particle (inertial position r and velocity v). ParticleDynamics returns its time derivative:

  • The binary follows the Hou equations of the full two-body problem. The particles do not enter them.

  • For each particle and each body: q = C^T (r - c) is the particle's position in the body's frame, g = field.value(q) is the body's gravity there, and C g is that acceleration in inertial axes. So

    dr/dt = v dv/dt = C_1 g_1(q_1) + C_2 g_2(q_2)

  • With a Sun in the environment, solar.py adds the solar tide and radiation pressure.

Step 6. The propagator (propagator.py)

propagate_particles integrates one batch. The integrator is DOP853 (adaptive, 8th order), with a relative tolerance of 1e-10 and absolute tolerances of 0.1 mm and 1e-8 m/s.

The event functions are the signed distances from each particle to each boundary: positive outside, zero on the boundary. The boundaries are the two surfaces and, when the gravity model is the Hou series, the two Brillouin spheres. One step of the propagator (Propagator.step) does this:

  1. Evaluate the event functions.
  2. A particle within the event tolerances of a boundary and approaching it is terminated there.
  3. The step size is limited so that no particle can cover more than 80% of its distance to a boundary in one step.
  4. Take the step.
  5. If an event function changed sign during the step, the time of the crossing is located by root finding (Brent's method) on the step's dense output. The propagation goes back to that time and that particle is terminated there. The others continue.
  6. The states at the regular output times (every cadence_s) are saved, from the dense output.

A particle's propagation therefore ends with one of these terminal events, or with none:

Terminal event Meaning
impact It reached a body's surface
domain_exit It entered a Brillouin sphere while the gravity model was the Hou series
grazing It left a surface and came back within the event tolerances
completed No terminal event by the final time

Step 7. What is kept (trajectories.py, _reduce and Trajectories)

After each batch, _reduce computes for every saved state the position in each body's fixed frame, q = C^T (r - c), and the altitude above each surface. For a particle that impacted it also computes the velocity relative to the rotating surface, C^T (v - v_body) - omega x q.

Trajectories holds all particles together and gives one value per particle:

Attribute Meaning
outcome "impact", "in_orbit", "escape" or "domain_exit"
impact_body Name of the body it hit, or ""
flight_time Time of flight [s]
v1 Speed at the initial time [m/s], in the frame of the table
v2 Speed at impact relative to the surface [m/s]
impact_point Where it hit, in that body's fixed frame [m]
impact_distance Straight-line distance from the site to the impact point [m]

A particle with no impact by the final time is in orbit or escaping. The test is the sign of its two-body energy about the barycentre at the final time, v^2 / 2 - G (m1 + m2) / r: positive means "escape", otherwise "in_orbit". The binary is treated as one point mass for this test only.

trajectories.positions(i, frame) gives the positions of particle i in a body's fixed frame, the inertial frame or the rotating frame. trajectories.save(folder) writes everything, and bp.load_trajectories(folder) reads it back, so figures can be redrawn without propagating again.

Step 8. Figures and animations (plots/)

plots/figures.py has one function per figure and plots/animation.py has animate. Select particles first, then draw them:

impacting = trajectories.impacting("Dimorphos")
bp.plot_trajectories(impacting, frame="Dimorphos", file="a.png")

Particles that impact the body they started from are best drawn in that body's fixed frame, the others in the rotating frame. The words on the figures are in plots/text.py (LABELS) and can be changed from your script.

python3 tools/dev.py test
