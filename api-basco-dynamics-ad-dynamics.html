basco.dynamics.ad.dynamics

The equations of motion of the binary (the full two-body problem), written with JAX.

The GUBAS state x holds, all with components in the primary's frame A:

r, v        position [km] and inertial velocity [km/s] of the secondary relative to the
            primary
wc, ws      spin vectors of the primary and of the secondary [rad/s]
Cc = C_NA   attitude of the primary (A to inertial)
C  = C_AB   attitude of the secondary relative to the primary (B to A)

With U(r, C) the mutual potential energy, m = M_A M_B / (M_A + M_B) the reduced mass and I_A, I_B the principal moments of inertia (as diagonal matrices), the equations are

dr/dt  = r x wc + v                           (the frame A rotates with wc)
dv/dt  = v x wc - (1/m) dU/dr
dCc/dt = Cc [wc x]
dC/dt  = C [C^T ws x] - [wc x] C
dwc/dt = I_A^-1 [ (I_A wc) x wc + M_A ]
dws/dt = (C I_B^-1 C^T) [ L_B x wc + M_B - dC/dt I_B C^T ws - C I_B d(C^T)/dt ws ]

M_B = - sum over the columns c_i of C of  c_i x dU/dc_i     torque on the secondary
M_A = r x dU/dr - M_B                                        torque on the primary
L_B = C I_B C^T ws                                           angular momentum of B

These are the lines of the function hou_ode of the GUBAS C++ code, in the same order. The one difference is where dU/dr and dU/dC come from: the C++ code has hand-derived expressions, and here JAX differentiates the potential. JAX can then differentiate this whole function once more, which gives the partials the variational equations need (partials.py).

The optional perturbations of hou_ode (flyby, heliocentric orbit, solar gravity, tides) are not included.

mass_properties

Import as basco.dynamics.ad.mass_properties

mass_properties(TA, TB, dim)

Principal moments of inertia of both bodies and the reduced mass, from the flat arrays of inertia integrals TA and TB:

I = (T_020 + T_002, T_200 + T_002, T_200 + T_020)        m = M_A M_B / (M_A + M_B)

They are computed here, inside the function that JAX differentiates, so that a change of a mass or of a second-degree integral reaches the equations of motion through the inertia and the reduced mass as well as through the potential.

Import as basco.dynamics.ad.equations_of_motion

equations_of_motion(x, TA, TB, G, potential)

The time derivative of the GUBAS state x (the equations at the top of this file).

TA, TB: flat arrays of the inertia integrals of the two bodies [kg km^degree]. G: gravitational constant in km^3 / (kg s^2). potential: a function U(r, C, TA, TB, G); its derivatives give the force and torques.
