basco.gravity.polyhedral

Gravity of a constant-density polyhedron (Werner and Scheeres 1996).

For a body bounded by flat triangular faces and filled with constant density sigma, the gravity at a point is an exact sum over the faces f and the edges e. With r_f the vector from the point to any point of face f, and r_e the same for edge e,

potential     U     = 1/2 G sigma [ sum_e (r_e . E_e r_e) L_e - sum_f (r_f . F_f r_f) w_f ]
acceleration  a     =    -G sigma [ sum_e (E_e r_e) L_e       - sum_f (F_f r_f) w_f ]
gradient      da/dr =     G sigma [ sum_e E_e L_e             - sum_f F_f w_f ]

F_f = n_f n_f^T                   n_f: outward unit normal of the face
E_e = n_A m_A^T + n_B m_B^T       A, B: the two faces that share the edge;
                                  m_A: unit vector in the plane of face A, perpendicular
                                  to the edge, pointing out of the face
L_e = ln((a + b + e) / (a + b - e))   a, b: distances from the point to the two ends
                                  of the edge; e: the edge's length
w_f = the solid angle of the face seen from the point, with a sign

In the paper U is positive. potential here is its negative, so it is negative, zero at infinity, and the acceleration is minus its derivative.

The formulas hold inside the body as well as outside. Potential and acceleration are continuous across the surface. The gradient jumps there and is refused on the surface itself.

Points are given in the frame of the mesh, in metres.

References: Werner and Scheeres, Celestial Mechanics and Dynamical Astronomy 65 (1996), doi 10.1007/BF00053511. Fu and Soldini, doi 10.1093/mnras/staf419, Eq. (1a-c), give the same expressions. Written from the papers, not from another implementation.

PolyhedralField

Import as basco.gravity.polyhedral.PolyhedralField

PolyhedralField(mesh: TriangleMesh, density: float | None = field(default=None, kw_only=True), mass: float | None = field(default=None, kw_only=True), G: float = field(default=G_SI, kw_only=True))

The gravity field of a TriangleMesh filled with constant density.

mesh: the shape, in metres. density: [kg/m^3], or instead mass: [kg]; the density is then mass / volume. Give exactly one of the two. G: gravitational constant.

field.value(point)        acceleration (3,) at one point
field.values(points)      accelerations (N, 3) at many points
field.potential(point)    potential at one point
field.evaluate(point)     PolyhedralEvaluation: potential, acceleration and gradient

Also available: mass, density, volume [m^3], center_of_mass and brillouin_radius (the largest distance of a vertex from the origin).

Everything that depends only on the mesh (the normals, the matrices F and E, the edge lengths) is computed once, when the field is made. Internally the mesh is shifted to its mean vertex and scaled to unit size, which keeps the arithmetic accurate; what goes in and comes out stays in the frame of the mesh and in metres.

Far from the body the face sum and the edge sum nearly cancel, so the accuracy slowly degrades with distance. A series (HouParticleField) is the better model far away.

PolyhedralField._compute

_compute(self, points, *, potential=False, gradient=False)

Acceleration at points (N, 3), and on request potential and gradient.

The steps follow the formulas at the top of this file:

  1. r: the vectors from each point to the three vertices of each face, and d = n_f . r, the height of the face's plane above the point.
  2. The solid angle w_f of each face, w = 2 atan2(numerator, denominator). Near an edge the usual denominator loses accuracy by cancellation, so it is rearranged there.
  3. The edge terms L_e, with a + b - e computed in a way that does not cancel.
  4. The sums over faces and edges.
