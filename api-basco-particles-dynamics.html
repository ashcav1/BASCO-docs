basco.particles.dynamics

Equations of motion of the binary and its particles, evaluated together.

The state vector is the binary followed by the particles:

state[:30]   the GUBAS state of the binary (relative position and velocity in km and
             km/s, the two spin vectors in rad/s and the two attitude matrices; see
             basco/binary/state.py)
state[30:]   six numbers per particle: inertial position r [m] and velocity v [m/s],
             measured from the binary's barycentre

ParticleDynamics(time, state) returns the time derivative of that vector.

The binary moves under its own mutual gravity (the Hou equations); the particles do not act on it. Each particle is pulled by both bodies. A body's gravity field is known in the body's own frame, so for a body with centre c and body-to-inertial rotation matrix C:

q = C^T (r - c)       the particle's position in the body's frame
g = field.value(q)    the body's gravitational acceleration there, in the body's frame
C g                   the same acceleration in inertial axes

The equations of motion of one particle are therefore

dr/dt = v
dv/dt = C_1 g_1(q_1) + C_2 g_2(q_2)  (+ the solar terms)

With solar forces (solar.py), the Sun pulls on the particles and on the binary, and sunlight pushes on the particles unless a body shadows them.

ParticleDynamics

ParticleDynamics(model, particles, *, fields=None, backend='ad', solar=None, surfaces=None, shadow='none')

The right-hand side of the equations of motion for one binary and N particles.

model: BinaryModel with Hou mutual gravity. particles: the Particle inputs (only their solar properties are used here). fields: {"A": field, "B": field}: gravity of each body on a particle. A body that is not given gets the exterior Hou series of the binary's own order. backend: "ad" or "analytic": how the binary's equations and partials are evaluated. solar: optional SolarForces. shadow: "none", "sphere" or "mesh". surfaces: {"A": surface, "B": surface} for impact detection and mesh shadows. A field that has a shape model (polyhedral gravity) supplies its own.

active marks the particles still being propagated; the propagator switches a particle off at its terminal event, and its derivative is zero from then on.

ParticleDynamics.illumination

illumination(self, epoch_s, poses, positions)

1 for each particle in sunlight and 0 for each one shadowed by a body.

The Sun is a point. "sphere" tests against each body's Brillouin sphere; "mesh" casts a ray toward the Sun against each body's shape.
