CubeSat descent and landing covariance

The next application is covariance analysis of a massless spacecraft in the full rotating binary system. The spacecraft has its own initial position and velocity. Both asteroids retain extended-body Hou dynamics; the spacecraft feels their fields but does not affect their motion. This is the restricted full three-body problem (RF3BP).

The spacecraft application is planned, not implemented in the public API. Existing consider analysis and covariance histories supply the statistical components. Copied legacy code demonstrates a nominal particle trajectory, release and contact event, but does not connect these to complete binary and spacecraft sensitivities.

This work is covariance only. A nominal trajectory is propagated for linearization. There is no generation of simulated measurements, parameter fitting or filter update. Starting uncertainty comes from an existing Hera covariance/consider result or a declared joint distribution, plus spacecraft initial-state uncertainty. It stays fixed during the forecast.

Implementation order

Phase New functionality Result
1. Spacecraft trajectory and covariance Six-state spacecraft initial conditions; Hou field from both bodies; full state/parameter sensitivities; initial/release uncertainty map. Position and velocity covariance histories along an RF3BP trajectory. No surface model needed yet.
2. First-contact covariance Surface gradients, event-time sensitivity, body-frame transforms and contact-output Jacobians. Contact-location ellipse, contact-time uncertainty and surface-relative velocity uncertainty.
3. Hera/Didymos application and validation Existing Hera uncertainty and declared release uncertainty; optional correlated trajectory-only ensemble. Reproducible descent covariance and checks of its linear approximation, without synthetic observations or estimation.

The detailed plan has acceptance checkboxes. The source audit identifies existing owners and gaps. Filters and measurement simulation are outside these phases.

Reuse existing BASCO components

Reuse Hou AD/native evaluators, physical parameter maps, the common-stage variational integrator, source covariance factors, consider cross covariance, arbitrary-Jacobian output histories, the FD checker, exports and plot theme. A second covariance solver or general uncertainty framework is unnecessary.

The missing connection is spacecraft dynamics and its derivatives. Initially integrate binary and spacecraft at common RK4 stages, avoiding a new interpolated ephemeris. The binary retains its 30 stored coordinates and 18 independent physical initial-error directions. The spacecraft adds six stored coordinates and six initial-error directions. Statistical parameters/nuisance coordinates remain separate named axes.

Phase 1: spacecraft trajectory and uncertainty

For binary state y, spacecraft state s = (r_N, v_N) and parameters p,

\dot y=f_B(y,p),\qquad
\dot s=\begin{bmatrix}v_N\\g_A(s,y,p)+g_B(s,y,p)\end{bmatrix}.

The state Jacobian includes binary-to-spacecraft dependence and zero spacecraft-to-binary dependence:

A=\begin{bmatrix}A_B&0\\A_{SB}&A_S\end{bmatrix}.

Use the same model for nominal and variational equations. Hou AD remains the default; explicit native selection must pass the same supported checks. The initial target retains order-6 gravity and parameter uncertainty through degree 4. Check integration steps for the spacecraft timescale rather than inheriting the Hera observing scenario's step.

The spacecraft input specifies six position/velocity values, units, frame, reference epoch and uncertainty. A body-fixed release includes body translation, rotation and omega cross rho in initial velocity. Its Jacobian creates correlations with the binary. Mass-dependent body COM locations and direct mass/gravity effects also enter the sensitivity map.

For all source errors q, let J(t) map them into reported spacecraft position/velocity. With source factor F, reuse the stable covariance map:

P(t)=(J(t)F)(J(t)F)^T.

Retain state-field, position-velocity and estimated-consider cross terms and unsupported directions. Do not reconstruct a lost factor from rounded covariance. Declare the source reference epoch and information cutoff; a forecast must not silently use later observations.

Phase 1 acceptance requires a short covariance runner, independent point-mass limits, no-backreaction checks, AD/native agreement, opt-in FD checks and integration refinement. No spacecraft observations are required.

Phase 2: contact time and surface position

Reuse surface geometry and first-crossing event values. Add gradients and the event-time correction. For g(t,z,q)=0, fixed-time state sensitivity Z and vector field f,

\tau_q=-\frac{g_zZ+g_q}{g_t+g_zf}.

For contact output h,

J_{\mathrm{contact}}=h_zZ+h_q+(h_t+h_zf)\tau_q.

Map the source factor through this Jacobian. Fixed-time position covariance at nominal contact omits uncertain contact time. Body-fixed position and surface-relative velocity also depend on target COM, orientation and spin. Validate smooth plane/sphere cases before an asteroid mesh, including independently terminated FD trajectories. Grazing or ambiguous contact requires a diagnostic rather than an unreliable finite ellipse.

Project contact-position covariance into a declared local tangent plane. Export its origin, normal and basis; label ellipse probability content explicitly. First contact does not describe bouncing or final rest.

Field and surface validity

Prioritize a Hou particle-field adapter and check units, sign and derivatives against independent limits. An exterior expansion accurate at the binary orbit is not automatically accurate at an irregular surface. Check spatial convergence before interpreting a footprint. The copied polyhedral field can provide a comparison where appropriate, with an explicit relationship to uncertain moments. A new mascon or Cartesian-multipole implementation is unnecessary.

Phase 3: validation without measurements

Use an existing Hera joint covariance/consider result and append declared release uncertainty. Compare mass/gravity-only, release-only and full cases, preserving correlations.

An optional ensemble draws correlated binary states, gravity parameters and spacecraft initial states, then propagates trajectories and determines each one's contact. It generates no measurements and performs no estimation. Compare empirical covariance/footprint coverage with the first-order prediction; record non-contact and other-body outcomes separately. This is an explicitly invoked linearization check, not part of each covariance run.

References

Tapley, Schutz and Born, Statistical Orbit Determination covers the classical orbit, linearization, square-root and consider topics; its publisher contents were reviewed. SCARABAEUS propagation documentation provides a reference for separating force models, integration and sensitivities. Fodde et al. provides a binary-landing uncertainty application; its author abstract was reviewed. These references inform the plan; they do not establish BASCO RF3BP validation.
