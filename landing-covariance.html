CubeSat descent and landing covariance

The planned application is a first-contact location covariance for a CubeSat descending onto Didymos or Dimorphos. Inputs will include the joint binary state, mass and gravity uncertainty from a Hera-like analysis, plus a declared release uncertainty. Outputs will include body-fixed contact location, contact time and surface-relative velocity.

This landing application is not implemented. Current consider analysis and covariance histories propagate uncertainty in the binary and its ideal observables. A binary relative-position or barycentric-position history does not describe a CubeSat trajectory or landing footprint. Copied legacy landing scripts are available as implementation references, not as a validated public BASCO landing API.

The first target is an unpowered, massless point spacecraft in the time-dependent binary field, with a rigid prescribed target surface and a first-contact event. Navigation updates, thrust, spacecraft attitude/shape, rebound and final rest location require later components. The implementation roadmap owns the complete staged scope and acceptance criteria.

Planned components and order

Each stage has one numerical responsibility and can be checked independently of plotting and estimation strategy. Public runners should compose these components rather than contain alternate force or covariance equations.

Stage Component Required checks
L1 Joint uncertainty record: labels, units, reference epoch, frames, attitude tangents, nominal values, full covariance or supported factor/nullspace, and provenance. Reordering and linear-map round trips; retention of state–field and estimated–consider cross terms; unsupported outputs remain unavailable.
L2 Binary background: positions, velocities, attitudes, angular rates and their joint-coordinate derivatives at requested epochs. Hou native/AD agreement, frame and epoch checks, rotation-preserving interpolation, refinement below the descent uncertainty budget and no extrapolation.
L3 Spacecraft field and restricted dynamics: both bodies' gravity, position/parameter derivatives and optional additive force components. Point-mass and sphere references, two-body superposition, frame invariance, optional finite differences and integration refinement.
L4 Release map and covariance transport: convert body, inertial or carrier-frame release conditions to spacecraft state and propagate the same model's sensitivities. Inertial/body-frame equivalence; direct and indirect mass/attitude dependence; complete cross terms; small-uncertainty sampled checks.
L5 Surface and first-contact event: geometry, normal and gradients, first outside-to-inside crossing, event time and output derivatives. Analytic plane/sphere cases; independently terminated finite-difference trajectories; rotating bodies; explicit grazing/edge failures and outcome classification.
L6 Footprint and contact reports: body-fixed and tangent-plane contact covariance, time, normal/tangential velocity, incidence angle and approach clearance. Frame invariance, probability-content ellipse tests, cross-covariance checks and explicit limits of one local Gaussian map.
L7 Nonlinear validation and Hera-like example: a seeded ensemble using the same background, release, field, surface and event interfaces. Empirical coverage, contact/no-contact/other-body-impact fractions with sampling uncertainty, outcome distributions and numerical refinement.

L1–L5 with a smooth sphere or ellipsoid provide the smallest useful validation case. L6–L7 are required before interpreting a plotted covariance as an asteroid landing prediction. A successful sphere case alone does not validate irregular-surface field accuracy or contact geometry.

Preserve the complete uncertainty

Let u be the declared input-error vector and Z(t) the fixed-time state Jacobian. Then

P_z(t)=Z(t)P_uZ(t)^T.

Pu must retain correlations among binary state, masses, moments, release conditions and any considered parameters. A consider result supplies the complete augmented error covariance, including estimated–consider blocks. Independently sampling estimated masses, moments and initial state would discard those correlations and define a different experiment.

Separate gravity-only and release-only contributions are meaningful when the relevant errors are independent by assumption. Otherwise the cross contribution belongs in the uncertainty budget and need not be positive by itself. Each exported study must state its independence assumptions, frames, epoch and covariance source.

Contact time is an uncertain output

A fixed-time position covariance at the nominal landing time is insufficient. Each perturbed trajectory generally reaches the surface at a different time.

For a smooth contact surface described by g(t,z,u) = 0, vector field f and nominal contact time tau, implicit differentiation gives

\tau_u=-\frac{g_zZ+g_u}{g_t+g_zf}.

For a reported event quantity h(t,z,u), the event Jacobian is

J_{\mathrm{event}}=h_zZ+h_u+(h_t+h_zf)\tau_u,\qquad
P_{\mathrm{event}}=J_{\mathrm{event}}P_uJ_{\mathrm{event}}^T.

The denominator is the nominal surface-crossing rate. Grazing contact makes this map ill-conditioned. Mesh edges, changing first-contact locations, no-contact outcomes or a different impacted body can invalidate a single smooth local derivative. Such cases need explicit diagnostics and nonlinear sampled outcomes, not a finite ellipse produced by ignoring the event.

Body-fixed contact position must also include uncertainty in target COM, attitude and contact time. Surface-relative velocity requires the target's translational and rotational motion at that same contact event. Post-contact bouncing and settling need an additional contact/reset model and its sensitivities.

Field and surface validity

Hou remains the coupled binary mutual-gravity model. A finite exterior moment expansion adequate for the mutual orbit is not automatically accurate near an irregular surface. The spacecraft field component needs a declared spatial validity domain, convergence/refinement evidence and a near-surface reference comparison.

Estimated moments do not uniquely determine an interior density or the local field near the surface. The mapping from estimated mass/moments to the chosen spacecraft field must therefore be explicit and differentiable. A uniform-density polyhedral field is one candidate nominal provider; scaling one shape's density is not equivalent to varying all independent estimated harmonic directions.

Geometry and mass distribution remain distinct inputs. Later surface uncertainty may include scale, shape modes, registration, COM-to-center-of-figure offsets, local normals and topography, with correlations to moments or density. Independently sampled meshes and moments can describe inconsistent bodies unless their statistical relationship is specified.

Footprint interpretation

For a smooth nominal contact point, a two-row orthonormal tangent basis T maps body-fixed contact covariance to

P_{\mathrm{surface}}=T P_{\mathrm{contact}}T^T.

The output must retain the tangent basis, origin and normal. A large, curved or multimodal footprint belongs on the surface geometry; a local planar ellipse may be inadequate.

For a locally Gaussian two-dimensional error distribution, a probability-content ellipse has semiaxes

a_i=\sqrt{\chi^2_2(p)\lambda_i}.

Here lambda_i are tangent-plane covariance eigenvalues and p is the stated probability content. A 95% ellipse uses a Mahalanobis radius of approximately 2.448. A radius of 3 contains approximately 98.89% in two dimensions. Label the probability explicitly; a footprint ellipse alone does not establish safe touchdown, rebound or final-rest probability.

Validation and reusable material

BASCO contains migration copies of the earlier restricted-three-body landing runner, surface helpers, impact events and example meshes. Before reuse, their units, geometry registration, rotation conventions, derivative support, field domains and event semantics must be checked. Cubic interpolation of individual attitude-matrix entries does not guarantee a valid rotation or consistent attitude sensitivities.

Acceptance will compare first-order predictions with independently event-terminated perturbed runs and a seeded ensemble. Each sample must find its own contact time and record contact, no contact, other-body impact or numerical failure. The nominal and sampled models must agree. Refinement must cover integration, background interpolation, gravity approximation and surface representation.

The initial Didymos–Dimorphos study should compare gravity-only, release-only and full correlated uncertainty budgets using the same modular components. Publication should include source identities, assumptions, numerical settings, covariance and sampled footprint, contact-condition distributions and uncertainty in empirical coverage.

The roadmap cites Celik et al., A comparative reliability analysis of ballistic deployments on binary asteroids, Fodde et al., Design and Analysis of Robust Ballistic Landings on the Secondary of a Binary Asteroid, and Kong et al., Saltation Matrices. These provide application and event-sensitivity references; none substitutes for implementing and validating BASCO's stated model.
