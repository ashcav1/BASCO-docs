basco.particles.trajectories

Propagate many particles and keep the trajectory and outcome of each one.

trajectories = propagate(environment, initial_conditions, site=site, duration_s=24 * 3600)

trajectories.outcome            "impact", "in_orbit", "escape" or "domain_exit", per particle
trajectories.impact_body        name of the body each particle hit ("" if it hit none)
trajectories.v1, .v2            speed at the initial time and at impact [m/s]
trajectories.positions(i, frame)   positions of particle i in a body-fixed frame, the
                                "inertial" frame or the "rotating" frame of the binary
trajectories.table()            one row per particle, for printing or saving

Each particle is massless: it feels the two bodies but does not move them, and particles do not feel each other. A particle's propagation ends when it reaches a body's surface. There is no bouncing after an impact.

propagate

Import as basco.particles.propagate

propagate(environment, initial_conditions, site=None, body=None, duration_s=86400.0, cadence_s=20.0, max_step_s=60.0, batch_size=4, workers=1, checkpoint=None, show_progress=True, label=None)

Propagate every particle of initial_conditions and return their Trajectories.

environment: the binary, its shapes and the gravity model (see environment.py). initial_conditions: InitialConditions (positions and velocities). site: the Site whose east-north-up frame the initial conditions are given in, with velocities relative to the surface. Or give body for a body-fixed frame, or neither for the inertial frame. duration_s: propagation time. cadence_s: time between saved states. max_step_s: largest integration step. batch_size: particles integrated together with one copy of the binary. Particles in a batch share their step sizes, so results change in the last digits with the batching. Use 1 for results that do not depend on it (slower). workers: processes to use; 1 does everything in this process. checkpoint: folder for finished batches, so an interrupted propagation can be resumed. label: what this set of particles is, for figure titles; the site name if not set.

_script_hidden_from_workers

_script_hidden_from_workers()

Let a plain script use worker processes without an if __name__ == "__main__": guard.

Python starts each worker by running the main script again, which would repeat the whole propagation inside every worker. The workers here only call functions of this module, so the script is hidden from them while they start.

Trajectories

Import as basco.particles.Trajectories

Trajectories(initial_conditions, site, origin_body, body_names, masses, G, shapes, counts, times, inertial, body_frame, altitudes, end_kind, impact_body_index, end_time, impact_velocity, impact_normal, binary_times, binary_states, settings, label, subset='All particles')

The trajectories of N particles and how each one ended.

Per particle (arrays of length N): names labels, as in the initial conditions outcome "impact"; "in_orbit" (no impact by the final time, and bound to the binary); "escape" (no impact, and unbound: positive two-body energy at the final time); or "domain_exit" (it entered a body's Brillouin sphere, where the Hou gravity series is not valid) impact_body name of the body it hit, or "" impact_body_index 0, 1 or -1 for the same flight_time time of flight [s]: from the initial time to impact, or to the final time v1 speed at the initial time [m/s], in the frame the initial conditions were given in v2 speed at impact relative to the surface [m/s]; NaN without impact impact_point (N, 3) where it hit, in that body's fixed frame [m]; NaN without impact impact_distance straight-line distance from the site to the impact point [m]; NaN unless it hit the body it started from

State histories are stored end to end: particle i owns the rows rows(i) of times (seconds since the initial time), inertial (position and velocity), body_frame (position in each body's fixed frame) and altitudes (altitude above each body's surface).

origin_body is the index of the body the particles started from (None for inertial initial conditions). shapes holds the two body shape models, for drawing.

label says what the particles are ("Bed 1 at the Dimorphos equator"); subset says which of them this object holds ("All particles", or what you passed to select).
