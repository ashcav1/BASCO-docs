How the covariance analysis works

This page follows a covariance analysis from the definition of the binary to a table of uncertainties, and says which file does each step. Read it next to the code: every heading names the file to open.

The code is in src/basco/covariance/, with the binary in src/basco/binary/ and the variational equations in src/basco/dynamics/. The shortest complete script is examples/covariance/basics/custom_binary.py.

What is computed

A covariance analysis asks: if these quantities were observed at these times with this noise, how well would they determine these parameters? No observation values are needed. What is needed is how each observation changes when each unknown changes (the partial derivatives), and the noise.

Write x for the unknowns and y for all observations. Near the nominal trajectory, dy = H dx, where H is the design matrix. If every row of H is divided by the noise sigma of its observation (whitening), the covariance of the least-squares estimate of x is

P = (H^T H + a priori information)^-1

The 1-sigma uncertainty of an unknown is the square root of its diagonal entry of P. These are formal uncertainties: they are as good as the dynamical model, the observation model and the noise that went in.

The script, line by line

import basco.covariance as bc

model = bc.BinaryModel(bc.BinarySystem(primary, secondary, state))
parameters = (
    bc.EstimatedParameters(model).estimate_masses().estimate_moments().estimate_initial_state()
)
observations = bc.ObservationSchedule.uniform(
    stop=6 * 3600.0, cadence=600.0,
    observation_types=[bc.RelativePosition(sigma=1.0), bc.BodyAttitude("secondary", 0.02)],
)
analysis = bc.CovarianceAnalysis(model, parameters, observations, bc.RK4(dt=60.0))
design = analysis.design_matrix()
result = design.covariance()
print(result.summary())
print(result.mass_properties().summary())
Line What you get File
BinaryModel The two bodies, their initial state, the order of the mutual gravity binary/models.py
EstimatedParameters The list of unknowns binary/parameters.py
ObservationSchedule What is observed, when, and how precisely covariance/observations.py
CovarianceAnalysis The inputs together covariance/analysis.py
design_matrix() The trajectory, its partials, and H dynamics/, covariance/design_matrix.py
covariance() P and what it means covariance/solvers.py, covariance/results.py
mass_properties() A report with sigmas binary/derived_parameters.py

design_matrix() is the expensive step, because it integrates. covariance() is linear algebra on the result and takes a fraction of a second, so noise, a priori information and the choice of estimated parameters can be changed without integrating again.

Step 1. The binary (binary/models.py, binary/state.py)

A body is described by its inertia integrals

T_lmn = integral of x^l y^m z^n dm

over the body in its own frame (centre of mass at the origin, principal axes). T_000 is the mass. UniformEllipsoid computes them in closed form; InertiaIntegrals takes a table, for example from a shape model (TriangleMesh.to_distribution).

BinaryState is the state at the initial time: the position and velocity of the secondary relative to the primary, both spin vectors, and both attitudes. While it is propagated it is stored as 30 numbers, the GUBAS state (binary/state.py lists them).

HouGravity(mutual_order) chooses how many terms of Hou's series for the mutual gravity are kept: 2, 4 or 6.

Step 2. The unknowns (binary/parameters.py)

EstimatedParameters(model) collects what is estimated:

Call Unknowns
estimate_masses() primary.mass, secondary.mass
estimate_moments(degrees=(2,)) The inertia integrals of that degree divided by the mass, such as primary.T200
estimate_harmonics(degrees=(3, 4)) Gravity harmonics such as primary.C30
estimate_initial_state() The initial state, as a deviation of 18 numbers: position, velocity, two spins and two small rotation vectors

Biases of an observation type (covariance/nuisance.py) are unknowns too.

The equations of motion use the inertia integrals themselves. The matrix d T / d parameter (ParameterSet.inertia_integral_jacobian) converts partials with respect to inertia integrals into partials with respect to the chosen parameters.

Above degree 2, estimate harmonics, not inertia integrals. There are more inertia integrals of a degree than harmonics, and the extra combinations do not affect the gravity outside the body, so no observation of the motion can determine them.

Step 3. The variational equations (dynamics/)

With the GUBAS state x and the equations of motion dx/dt = F(x, p):

dPhi/dt = A Phi,        Phi(0) = I       Phi = dx(t) / dx(0), the state transition matrix
dPsi/dt = A Psi + B,    Psi(0) = 0       Psi = dx(t) / dp, the parameter sensitivities

A = dF/dx        B = dF/dp

These are integrated together with the trajectory by fixed-step RK4 (dynamics/integration.py). The result is a VariationalSolution with x, Phi and Psi at every observation epoch.

A and B come from one of two places, chosen with partials=:

partials= Where A and B come from File
"ad" (default) Automatic differentiation of the equations of motion written with JAX dynamics/ad/
"analytic" The hand-derived expressions of the GUBAS code dynamics/analytic.py, _legacy/

dynamics/ad/dynamics.py has the equations of motion themselves, written out.

Step 4. The observations (covariance/observations.py)

Each observation type gives the partials of its quantity y with respect to the GUBAS state, Hy, and any direct dependence on the parameters:

Type y
RelativePosition Position of the secondary relative to the primary, inertial axes
BodyAttitude Orientation of one body, as a small rotation vector
PrimaryBarycentricPosition Position of the primary about the barycentre; depends on the mass ratio directly

The epochs are every cadence up to duration. A schedule (covariance/schedules.py) can remove epochs, for tracking passes or blackouts, for all types or for one.

Step 5. The design matrix (covariance/design_matrix.py)

For an observation at epoch t_k:

dy / d(initial state deviation) = Hy Phi_k J
dy / d(bias coefficients)       = the observation type's own partials
dy / d(physical parameters)     = Hy Psi_k + direct

J is d(GUBAS state) / d(state deviation) at the initial time. These rows, stacked over all epochs and types, are H. The unknowns always refer to the initial time.

Step 6. Noise and a priori information (covariance/noise.py, priors.py, kaula.py)

Whitening divides each row by its noise sigma (or, for correlated noise with covariance R = L L^T, multiplies by L^-1).

A priori information is added as extra rows. Knowing a quantity J x with covariance P0 = L L^T adds the rows L^-1 J. APrioriCovariance does this for chosen unknowns; KaulaPrior does it for the gravity harmonics from a Kaula rule sigma_l = K / l^alpha (covariance/kaula.py fits such a rule to perturbed copies of a shape).

Without a priori information for an unknown, it is determined by the observations alone or not at all.

Step 7. The covariance (covariance/solvers.py)

The whitened rows A (observations on top of a priori rows) are never multiplied into A^T A, which would square the condition number. Instead:

  1. Each column is scaled by the typical size of its unknown.
  2. The singular values of the scaled rows are computed. One that is smaller than rank_rtol times the largest marks a direction about which the observations say nothing usable: an unobservable direction.
  3. With full rank, a QR factorization gives the covariance.
  4. Without full rank there is no covariance of all unknowns. BASCO then reports which directions are unobservable, and gives a sigma only to quantities that do not depend on them. It does not invent a finite sigma for the others.

PartitionedSquareRootSolver (covariance/partitioned_solver.py) first eliminates the initial state and the biases and judges the rank on the physical parameters alone. Use it for long arcs of chaotic rotation, where the columns of the initial state grow by many orders of magnitude.

Step 8. The result (covariance/results.py, binary/derived_parameters.py)

Call What it gives
result.summary() The ranks, and whether a full covariance exists
result.joint_covariance P for all unknowns, or None without full rank
result.covariance_for(labels) The block of P for the named unknowns
result.mass_properties() Masses, GM, total mass and mass ratio, with sigmas
result.degree2() Second-degree quantities (C20, C22, Izz), with sigmas
result.harmonics() Gravity harmonics, with sigmas
result.derived(labels, values, units, jacobian) Any quantity q, from its partials: J P J^T

bc.export_analysis(design, result, folder) saves everything, with tables in CSV, Markdown and LaTeX (covariance/exports.py).

Where to change things

To change Open
A body or the initial state binary/models.py; for Didymos, binary/didymos.py

An observation type, or add one covariance/observations.py; it needs labels, units, sigma and observation_partials

The equations of motion dynamics/ad/dynamics.py, equations_of_motion
How rank is decided covariance/solvers.py
A report binary/derived_parameters.py
A figure covariance/plots/render.py

After a change that should not alter numbers:

python3 tools/dev.py test

python3 tools/dev.py snapshot save after

python3 tools/dev.py snapshot compare before-restructure after

The last command must report that all items are identical.
