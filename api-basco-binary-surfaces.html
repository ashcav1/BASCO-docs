basco.binary.surfaces

Surfaces of a body, for impact detection: a plane, a sphere or a closed triangle mesh.

A surface answers one question, surface.evaluate(point): how far is this point (in the body's frame, metres) from the surface, and which way is out? The answer is a SurfaceSample with the signed distance (positive outside, negative inside), the outward unit normal and the nearest point of the surface. The propagator uses the signed distance as its event function: an impact is where it reaches zero.

TriangleSurface also finds where a ray or a segment first crosses the mesh.

smooth in the results says whether the distance is differentiable at that place. It is inside a face. On an edge or at a vertex the normal jumps from one face to the next, so first-order covariance of an impact point is not defined there.

TriangleSurface

Import as basco.covariance.trajectory.TriangleSurface

TriangleSurface(mesh: TriangleMesh, edge_tolerance: float = 1e-07)

Contact geometry for a closed outward-wound TriangleMesh. Solid angles provide inside/outside sign; nearest-face/edge geometry provides distance and normal. A face-interior chart is required for smooth covariance. Mesh edges/vertices remain explicit geometric outcomes. Self-intersections remain the caller's shape responsibility.

Distances to a closed TriangleMesh, and ray crossings of it.

The distance from a point is the smallest distance to any face: to the face's interior or to one of its edges. Whether the point is inside or outside comes from the solid angle that the whole surface covers as seen from the point: 4 pi from inside, 0 from outside.

edge_tolerance: a nearest point or a crossing closer to an edge than this (as a barycentric weight) is reported as not smooth. It does not change any distance.

The mesh must not pass through itself; that is not checked.

TriangleSurface.supporting_face

supporting_face(self, direction=None, *, minimum_alignment=0.8)

Choose a real mesh face whose plane contains every vertex on or behind it. Prefer the largest centroid-centred interior disk among sufficiently aligned normals; otherwise select the most aligned support. The returned clearance is face-interior geometry, not a body-wide safe impact radius. This permits a mass-free cylinder above the supporting plane without hardcoded asteroid face indices.

A face the body could rest on, facing direction (+z by default).

A supporting face has every vertex of the mesh on or behind its plane. Among those whose normal is within minimum_alignment (a cosine) of the direction, the one with the largest clearance radius is returned; if there is none, the best aligned supporting face.
