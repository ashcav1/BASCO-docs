basco.binary.models

The binary: two bodies, their state at the initial time and the order of their mutual gravity.

primary = Body("primary", UniformEllipsoid(axes=[400, 350, 300], density=2200))
secondary = Body("secondary", UniformEllipsoid(axes=[90, 75, 60], density=2200))
state = BinaryState(position, velocity, omega_primary, omega_secondary, A_to_N, B_to_A)
model = BinaryModel(BinarySystem(primary, secondary, state), HouGravity(mutual_order=4))

Inputs are plain numbers in SI units, or astropy quantities.

A body's mass distribution enters the equations of motion only through its inertia integrals

T_lmn = integral of x^l y^m z^n dm        [kg m^(l+m+n)]

taken over the body in its own frame. T_000 is the mass. T_200, T_020 and T_002 give the principal moments of inertia: I_xx = T_020 + T_002, and so on. A body frame has its origin at the centre of mass and its axes along the principal axes of inertia, so the integrals of degree 1 and the products T_110, T_101 and T_011 are zero.

"A" is the primary, "B" the secondary and "N" the inertial frame, as in Hou's equations.

UniformEllipsoid

Import as basco.covariance.UniformEllipsoid

UniformEllipsoid(axes: object, density: object)

A homogeneous ellipsoid with positive semi-axes in metres and density in kg/m³. Supplies analytic mass-weighted moments through degree 6; estimating independent moments does not constrain the uncertain body to remain homogeneous.

A body of constant density with semi-axes (a, b, c) along x, y, z.

axes: three semi-axes [m]. density: [kg/m^3].

Its inertia integrals are known in closed form. With mass M = 4/3 pi a b c density, T_lmn is zero when l, m or n is odd, and otherwise

T_lmn = M a^l b^m c^n * 3 (l-1)!! (m-1)!! (n-1)!! / (l+m+n+3)!!

so for example T_200 = M a^2 / 5.

InertiaIntegrals

Import as basco.covariance.InertiaIntegrals

InertiaIntegrals(inertia_integrals: object, reference_radius: object, brillouin_radius: object, characteristic_length: object = None, available_degree: object = None, missing: str = 'error')

Explicit centered principal-axis mass moments in SI. The reference radius fixes harmonic normalization; the bounding radius supports non-overlap checks; characteristic_length sets numerical coordinate scales. Missing moments are errors unless missing="zero" is deliberately selected.

A body given directly by a table of its inertia integrals.

inertia_integrals: {(l, m, n): T_lmn} in kg m^(l+m+n), in the body's frame (centre of mass at the origin, principal axes). reference_radius: reference radius of the spherical harmonics [m]. brillouin_radius: radius of the smallest sphere about the centre of mass that contains the body [m]. It is not the reference radius. characteristic_length: length that scales the integrals when they are estimated [m]; the Brillouin radius when not given. available_degree: highest degree l + m + n in the table, 2 to 6. missing: "error": every integral through that degree must be given. "zero": integrals that are not given are zero.

The mass T_000 and the three second moments T_200, T_020, T_002 must be positive. The integrals of degree 1 and the products of inertia must be zero or absent.

BinaryState

Import as basco.covariance.BinaryState

BinaryState(position: object, velocity: object, omega_primary: object, omega_secondary: object, A_to_N: object, B_to_A: object)

Position and inertial velocity are relative-secondary-to-primary vectors expressed in primary-body components. Velocity is not d(position_in_primary_frame)/dt. Both inertial spin vectors are also expressed in primary components. A_to_N maps primary components to inertial components; B_to_A maps secondary components to primary components. Public lengths are metres, rates radians/s, and rotation matrices dimensionless.

The state of the binary at one time, in SI units.

position: centre of the secondary relative to the centre of the primary [m], components in the primary's frame A. velocity: inertial velocity of the secondary relative to the primary [m/s], components in A. This is the inertial rate of change of the relative position, resolved in A; it is not the rate of change of the three numbers in position, which also change because A rotates. omega_primary, omega_secondary: inertial spin vectors [rad/s], both with components in A. A_to_N: rotation matrix from A components to inertial components. B_to_A: rotation matrix from the secondary's frame B to A.

Both matrices must be rotations (orthogonal, determinant +1).

HouGravity

Import as basco.covariance.HouGravity

HouGravity(mutual_order: int = 2)

The maintained binary gravity workflow uses total mutual expansion order 2, 4 or 6. This nominal order is separate from the estimated moment/harmonic degrees, which currently run through degree 4.

Mutual gravity by Hou's series, truncated at total order mutual_order (2, 4 or 6).

The mutual potential is a sum of terms, each the product of an inertia integral of A and one of B. The order of a term is the sum of the two degrees. Order 2 keeps the masses and the second-degree integrals; orders 4 and 6 add finer detail of both shapes.
