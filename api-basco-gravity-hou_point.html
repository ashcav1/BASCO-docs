basco.gravity.hou_point

Gravity of one extended body on a point particle, from Hou's series.

Hou's series gives the mutual potential of two bodies A and B from the inertia integrals of both. Take the asteroid as A and, as B, a point of unit mass, whose only nonzero inertia integral is T_000 = 1. The mutual potential is then the asteroid's potential per unit mass at that point, and minus its gradient is the gravitational acceleration there. So the series that moves the binary also gives a body's pull on a particle, with no new mathematics.

Everything here is in SI units and in the asteroid's own frame. The series converges only outside the body's Brillouin sphere. That check is made by HouParticleField in fields.py, not here.

HouBodyField.value(position) gives the acceleration. evaluate(position, indices) also gives its partial derivatives with respect to the position and to chosen inertia integrals, which the covariance of a particle trajectory needs.

HouBodyField

Import as basco.covariance.trajectory.HouBodyField

HouBodyField(moments: object, order: int = dataclass_field(kw_only=True), G: float = dataclass_field(kw_only=True), backend: str = dataclass_field(default='ad', kw_only=True), _flat: object = dataclass_field(init=False, repr=False, compare=False), _analytic: object = dataclass_field(default=None, init=False, repr=False, compare=False))

Advanced local SI field adapter: the asteroid is the extended Hou body, the receiver is a unit monopole. Acceleration, position gradient and selected mass-weighted raw-moment derivatives are distinct from normalized physical parameter partials. The dynamics caller owns frame transforms, exterior bounds and the raw-to-physical chain rule.

Hou-series gravity of one body on a point.

moments: {(l, m, n): T_lmn}, the body's inertia integrals [kg m^(l+m+n)] in its own frame. T_000 is the mass. Integrals that are not given are zero. order: 2, 4 or 6: the highest degree of inertia integral used. G: gravitational constant [m^3/(kg s^2)]. backend: "ad" (automatic differentiation with JAX, the default) or "analytic" (the hand-derived gradient and second derivatives of the potential). One never stands in for the other.

Example:

field = HouBodyField({(0, 0, 0): 5e11}, order=6, G=6.674e-11, backend="analytic") result = field.evaluate([1500.0, 0.0, 0.0], indices=((0, 0, 0),)) result.acceleration.shape, result.moment_jacobian.shape ((3,), (3, 1))

HouBodyField.evaluate

evaluate(self, position, indices=())

The acceleration with its partial derivatives, as a FieldEvaluation.

indices lists the inertia integrals (l, m, n) to differentiate with respect to. Each gives one column of moment_jacobian, in that order. The derivative exists also for an integral whose value is zero.

The acceleration is linear in the inertia integrals. The analytic backend therefore gets each column as the acceleration of a body that has only that one integral, set to 1.
