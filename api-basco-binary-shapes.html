basco.binary.shapes

Shape models: a closed triangle mesh, its inertia integrals, and families of perturbed shapes.

Lengths are in metres. TriangleMesh is what the particle code and the polyhedral gravity use. RadialShapeSampler and DensityVariation make the families of rough shapes and of non-uniform densities from which Kaula rules are fitted (basco/covariance/kaula.py).

Integrals over the volume (the volume itself, the centre of mass, the inertia integrals) are sums over the tetrahedra formed by the origin and each face. A face whose outward normal points toward the origin counts negatively, which makes the sum right for concave shapes too. That needs every face to be wound the same way, outward, which TriangleMesh checks. The integration routine is the one of GUBAS, kept in basco/_legacy.

TriangleMesh

Import as basco.covariance.TriangleMesh

TriangleMesh(vertices_m: np.ndarray, faces: np.ndarray, provenance: dict = field(default_factory=dict))

A closed, outward-wound triangular surface in metres with provenance. Topology/volume checks do not certify absence of every possible self-intersection or interior-region containment.

A closed surface made of triangles.

vertices_m: (V, 3) vertex positions [m]. faces: (F, 3) the three vertex numbers of each triangle, counter-clockwise when seen from outside. provenance: where the mesh came from (file, checksum, scale), kept for the records.

Checked on creation: every edge belongs to exactly two faces that run along it in opposite directions (the surface is closed and consistently wound), no face has zero area, and the volume is positive (the winding is outward). Not checked: faces that pass through each other. The arrays cannot be modified afterwards.

TriangleMesh.to_distribution

to_distribution(self, mass_kg, *, max_degree=6, reference_radius_m=None, alignment_tolerance=1e-10)

Align to center of mass/principal axes, integrate mass-weighted moments, check structural roundoff, and produce a Hou-ready InertiaIntegrals. The fixed harmonic radius and enclosing bounding radius have distinct meanings.

The InertiaIntegrals of this shape with constant density and total mass mass_kg, in the form a Body takes.

The shape is first moved to its body frame (aligned). The integrals of degree 1 and the products of inertia are then zero up to rounding; they are set to exactly zero after checking that they are below alignment_tolerance relative to mass * radius^degree. The reference radius is that of the sphere of equal volume unless given, and the Brillouin radius is the largest distance of a vertex from the centre of mass.

RadialShapeSampler

Import as basco.covariance.RadialShapeSampler

RadialShapeSampler(roughness_m: float, correlation_lengths_m: tuple, copies_per_length: int = 50, seed: int = 0, stream: int = 0, legacy_integer_length_seed: bool = False)

Seeded radial shape perturbations with requested RMS roughness and correlation lengths in metres. Choose sample count, resolution, frame/realignment and legacy seed convention explicitly for a science campaign.

Random rough versions of a shape, for fitting a Kaula rule.

Every vertex is moved along its radial direction by a random field built from spherical harmonics without degrees 0 and 1, scaled to an RMS of roughness_m. A correlation length L sets the highest degree of the field: max(2, round(pi R / L)), with R the reference radius.

roughness_m: RMS radial displacement [m]. correlation_lengths_m: one family of shapes is made for each length [m]. copies_per_length: shapes per family. seed, stream: random seeds; the same values give the same shapes. legacy_integer_length_seed: True reproduces the random numbers of the Hera paper, which seeded with the integer part of the correlation length.

DensityVariation

Import as basco.covariance.DensityVariation

DensityVariation(kind: str, contrast: float, core_radius_fraction: float = 0.8, shell_scale: float = 0.8, torus_ring_fraction: float = 0.75, torus_tube_fraction: float = 0.1, torus_segments: tuple = (128, 64))

Mass-preserving core/shell/torus interior alternatives for coefficient ensembles. Verify that chosen regions lie inside your body; the generic mesh does not prove that containment.

A region of different density inside a shape, with the total mass kept the same.

kind: "core" a sphere at the centre, of radius core_radius_fraction * R; "shell" a surface layer: the shape minus a copy of itself scaled by shell_scale; "torus" a ring in the equatorial plane, of ring radius torus_ring_fraction * R and tube radius torus_tube_fraction * R. contrast: density of the region relative to the rest, minus one (0.2 is 20% denser).

R is the reference radius given to moments. Whether a core or torus lies inside an irregular shape is for the user to check.
