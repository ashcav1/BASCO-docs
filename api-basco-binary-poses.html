basco.binary.poses

Where the two bodies are: position, velocity, rotation and spin of each one, from a GUBAS state.

The GUBAS state holds the secondary relative to the primary. The particle code needs each body by itself, in inertial axes with the origin at the binary's barycentre. With r the relative position in metres and m_A, m_B the masses,

c_A = -m_B / (m_A + m_B) C_NA r        c_B = +m_A / (m_A + m_B) C_NA r

and the same for the velocities. The body-to-inertial rotation matrices are C_NA for the primary and C_NA C_AB for the secondary.

The attitude matrices are used exactly as stored. A fixed-step integrator lets them drift slightly from perfect rotations, and nothing here corrects that, so these functions and their partial derivatives describe exactly the numbers that were propagated.

The functions ending in _jacobians give the partial derivatives of the same quantities with respect to the 30 entries of the GUBAS state and the two masses. The covariance of a particle trajectory uses them; the particle simulation does not.

_rotation_rates

_rotation_rates(state)

C_NA, C_AB, two skew matrices and the time derivatives of C_NA and C_AB.

dC_NA/dt = C_NA [omega_A x]
dC_AB/dt = C_AB [C_AB^T omega_B x] - [omega_A x] C_AB

[w x] is the skew matrix of w, so that [w x] p = w x p. Both spins have components in A. These are the expressions in the binary's equations of motion.

Return the two barycentric body poses plus their coded body-to-inertial rotation rates. COM velocity uses the retained inertial-relative-velocity convention. Both native30 DCM continuation and the secondary product rule remain explicit.

Both bodies as BodyKinematics: the poses and the rates of change of their rotations.

Primary: dC/dt = dC_NA/dt. Secondary: C = C_NA C_AB, so dC/dt = dC_NA/dt C_AB + C_NA dC_AB/dt.
