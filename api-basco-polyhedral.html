basco.polyhedral

Homogeneous polyhedral gravity in the supplied mesh frame, using SI units.

Independent implementation of the face/edge dyads of Werner and Scheeres, DOI 10.1007/BF00053511 (1996). Equations (1a--c) in Fu and Soldini, DOI 10.1093/mnras/staf419, give the same positive geodetic potential; here potential is its negative, acceleration is attractive, and gradient is the derivative of acceleration. Sources retrieved 2026-11-09. No code is copied from a third-party implementation or from the retained legacy kernel.

This is a constant-density nominal particle field, including interior trial points. It supplies no shape, source-coefficient, or covariance partials. Mesh self-intersections are not detected by TriangleMesh and remain an input-quality responsibility. A geometric impact event belongs to the trajectory caller.

Specific potential [m²/s²], acceleration [m/s²], and gradient [s⁻²].

Potential is negative with zero at infinity. gradient[i,j] is d(acceleration[i])/d(position[j]); it is NOT the Hessian of that negative potential. Arrays are owned, finite and read-only.

PolyhedralField

Import as basco.polyhedral.PolyhedralField

PolyhedralField(mesh: TriangleMesh, density: float | None = field(default=None, kw_only=True), mass: float | None = field(default=None, kw_only=True), G: float = field(default=G_SI, kw_only=True))

Closed-form uniform-density field of a checked TriangleMesh.

Exactly one of density [kg/m³] and mass [kg] must be supplied. Coordinates are those of mesh [m]; no mesh translation or rotation is applied to the physical model. require_body_frame can enforce a COM/principal-axis contract before attaching this field to binary dynamics.

Potential and acceleration have continuous limits on faces, edges and vertices. At a numerically resolved edge the vanishing E r log term is evaluated by its zero limit. The gradient is rejected on the entire density boundary, within 64 machine epsilons of the mesh radius, because no single two-sided derivative exists. Away from this tolerance the exact face/edge expressions are used, including their near-edge logarithmic behavior.

Precomputed geometry is reused; batch evaluation bounds scratch storage. As in other face/edge closed forms, far-field cancellation eventually limits floating-point accuracy. No multipole fallback or approximation is substituted. The caller must validate its requested distance range.
